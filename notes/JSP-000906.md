## Proof and corrections to the supplied sketches

1. Construct all k-subset indicator vectors in ℝ^(d+1). Injectivity gives `choose (d+1) k` points. Squared pairwise distance is twice the size of the set difference, giving at most k positive distances.
2. The coordinate-sum functional has a dimension-d kernel. Translate the affine hyperplane to this kernel and use an orthonormal basis to construct `constantSumIsometryEquiv`, a bijective isometry with ℝ^d. This proves the lower construction in the actual catalog space, not merely an unspecified dimension-d space.
3. The degree-≤s polynomial space has dimension `choose (d+s) s`: bounded exponent vectors correspond to multisets through a slack coordinate. This is `JSP906.finrank_restrictTotalDegree`.
4. The distance kernel has joint degree 2s. If a linear combination of degree-s evaluation functionals vanishes, its weights annihilate the weighted double sum of every degree-2s monomial: one of its two variable blocks has degree at most s. On the given point set the kernel is diagonal with a constant nonzero diagonal. Its double sum is that constant times a sum of squares, forcing all weights to vanish. Linear independence in the dual polynomial space yields BBS, `JSP906.BBS.card_le_choose`, and then threshold existence and the upper bound.
5. `isEquivalent_choose` gives the leading term for fixed shifted binomial coefficients. Divide by d^k and squeeze using both bounds. The limit is fully proved, not statement-only.

The unconditional instruction to show exactly k=n−1 distances is false for small dimensions. The delivered `JSP906.indicatorSet_distances_card` proves exactly k under `2*k ≤ m` in ambient dimension m; the at-most-k bound holds for all m,k and suffices for the catalog bound. For example k=m>0 gives only one point and zero distances. The supplied assertion that the original distance polynomials have degree at most s is also false; the duality argument above repairs it without weakening the bound.
